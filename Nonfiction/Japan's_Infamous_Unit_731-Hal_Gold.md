# Japan's Infamous Unit 731: First-hand Accounts of Japan's Wartime Human Experimentation Program - Hal Gold

## Summary
### Part 1 - Historial Overview
* The Japanese soldiers that lose to diseases are higher than bullets during the war with China in 1894, which has taught them the lesson of placing priority on disease prevention and treatment. They implemented an effective sanitation system, and every base and field hospital in Manchuria has its bacteriological laboratory. 
* By the beginning of the next century, Japanese military medicine and wartime bacteriology were the best in the world.
* Japanese success in minimising deaths from illness proved that they were correct to treat both germs and bullets with equal importance.
* Ishii Shiro pressed for the development of bacteriological research as a weapon for offensive warfare.
* The Mukden incident led to the Japanese taking control of Manchuria and setting a stage for the procurement of human specimens for the lab of Unit 731.
* The elite group of military police known as kenpeitai arrested anti-Japanese individuals, patriots who formed underground groups to oppose Japanese occupation and resistance members. The kenpeitai was responsible for taking the prisoners and sending them for the experiments conducted by Unit 731.
* The Japanese, led by Ishii, built a large building with a hundred rooms in a poor neighbourhood near Beiyinhe, Harbin. They also built a restricted military zone and a Japanese military airport. Everything was in secrecy, and the Chinese labourers were under constant watch by Japanese guards. The complex consists of offices, living quarters, dining areas, warehouses, a parking lot, prisons, laboratories, crematoria, and an area for munitions storage. The new facility has a 3-metre-high wall topped with barbed wire and high-voltage electric doors swung open to a drawbridge. The road in front of the facility was off-limits to the citizens. Trains passing by on rails about a kilometre away were required to put their shades down.
* Experiments such as taking blood samples from the prisoners were more of a combination of professional curiosity than of actual science. Some were injected with poison when they could no longer serve as lab materials. Subjects which are too weak to offer physical resistance would be killed by an axe, and the brain might be used for further research.
* A new site was built in the late 1930s at Pingfang, closer to the city of Harbin. The airspace over the area was off-limits to all aircraft other than Japanese army planes.
* Vivisection gave researchers the ability to examine the organs of a living person to see the progress of a disease.
* Pingfang became a virtual place to supply specimens to the Tokyo medical school. Unit 731 was performing the service of human experimentation for the entire Japanese medical community. There were units set up in Beijing, Nanjing, Guangzhou, and Singapore.
* Other satellite facilities are:
  * Anda (an open-air testing ground by exposing human beings to pathogenic substances in open-air conditions).
  * Xinjing (research on pathogens effective against domesticated animals, creating bacteria, mixing poisons with food to study their effects on subjects, and research into chemicals for crop destruction).
  * Guangzhou (raised rats for experiments in spreading plague, starving prisoners to death, and the germ warfare unit called Nami Unit 8604 is located here).
  * Beijing (Unit 1855, typhus test).
  * Singapore (Oka 9420 is located in this facility and focuses on creating plague as a weapon).
  * Hiroshima (producing poison gas for chemical warfare).
* Around 1945, when the end of the war was coming, Japan blew up the facilities of Unit 731 and its branch units to destroy evidence of their existence.
* After the war, the United States granted immunity to the Ishii's group because they wanted to obtain the data to grow their own biological weapons and also prevent the data falls into the Soviet Union.

### Part 2 - Testimonies
The second part presents the words of former unit members themselves, taken from what they recount of their firsthand memories of their participation in horrific experiments on men, women, and children. Their remarks were gathered during the Unit 731 exhibition held in Japan in 1994-1995.

## Personal Thoughts
* The length of this book is short, but the content is heavy.
* Thought-provoking and thoroughly researched. I wish the writer had added some sensory details for the second part because it was told in a flat tone.
