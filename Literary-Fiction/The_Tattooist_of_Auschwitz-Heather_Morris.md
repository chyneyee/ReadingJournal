# The Tattooist of Auschwitz (The Tattooist of Auschwitz #1) - Heather Morris

## Summary
In 1942, Lale and other young men were transported to a mysterious place in a cattle train for the sake of the safety of their families. Following Hitler's order, the Slovakian government has demanded that each Jewish family hand over a child aged eighteen or older to work for the German government. When Lale arrives in Birkenau (also known as Auschwitz II), a string of numbers is tattooed on his left forearm before being put into a crowded prison. Lale begins to work in the concentration camp as a builder. Then, Lale's kapo, or boss, offers Lale to be his helper. Later, Lale encounters Pepan, a tattooist in the camp who saves Lale's life when Lale is thrown into a body cart. Pepan offers Lale to become his assistant.

One day, Lale is promoted to become the tattooist after Pepan mysteriously disappears. A young man called Leon becomes his assistant. A tattooist is given a separate room and an extra food allowance, and Lale sneaks some extra food for other inmates who lived with him in the previous block. Lale befriends his superior, who is a year younger than him. His superior is proud and cruel to the prisoners but immature due to his age.

The first time Lale needs to tattoo the numbers on this particular girl, he has already set his eyes on her. Lale gets to know that her name is Gita, and they stealthily meet on Sundays.

<br>

## Personal Thoughts
* I was preparing for a heavy read and never expected the storytelling to be straightforward and easy to read.
* I feel the story has a lot of potential, but the weak writing style fails to accentuate the beauty of the story, and the intended impact isn't there. It seems the writer is attempting to convey a touching story, but it's very hard to get moved by the lacking depth. I believe the story has the potential to offer deeper meanings, but the narrative style only provides surface-level messages.
