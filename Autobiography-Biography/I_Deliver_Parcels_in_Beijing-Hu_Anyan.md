# I Deliver Parcels in Beijing: On Making a Living - Hu Anyan, Jack Hargreaves (Translator)

## Summary
- The writer recalls the difficulties with the bureaucratic process of getting hired as a delivery service worker and the competitions among the massive number of courier companies in China.
- Slacking colleagues, unreasonable customers, and the ugliness of humanity from the writer's perspective.
- Interesting to know some of the delivery systems in China. For example, a customer who bought something from the teleshop channels would want to try on it when they first arrived. The delivery worker has to wait for the customer to finish trying the purchased item. If the customer cancelled the order after that on the spot, the delivery worker didn't receive any commission and had to repackage the item. 
- The writer voices out the concern of a dodgy purchase to prevent an elderly woman from being scammed. If the customer chooses not to confirm the order, the item will be returned, and the delivery worker won't earn any commissions from it. 
- The writer shares his experience of working as a delivery service worker and various odd jobs, including selling a bike and opening his own small business. In the later part, he tells how he became a writer.

### Personal Thoughts
- I need to get myself to adapt to the structure of the sentence because of the translation from the Chinese. I found it difficult to follow the story due to the structure of sentences, but it feels very precise in how Chinese sentences were written.
- The narrated experience is very genuine and raw. It's very straightforward, and there's no hiding of his feelings about problematic companies or unreasonable customers. 
- Customers seem to always overlook the procedures of the delivery service and always expect their parcels to be the highest priority. The customers always think that their time is more valuable than the delivery workers', and the delivery workers have to follow their demands since they paid for the service. I bet most people are unaware that the delivery workers usually didn't receive the wage equivalent to the effort or hours that they worked.
