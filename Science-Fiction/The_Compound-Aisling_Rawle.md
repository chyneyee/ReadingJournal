# The Compound - Aisling Rawle

## Summary
The narrator, Lily, wakes up in a compound in the middle of the desert, along with nine other women. While waiting for the men to arrive, they decide to tidy up the place. Cameras are tracking their move in the compound, broadcasting to millions of reality TV fans. There are nine men arriving the next day. The women have to share a bed with a man, respectively, if they want to stay in the competition. Since there are ten women in the compound, one of them would be banished if she is not good enough to win a man's heart to share a bed with her.

There are rules on the compound. The contestants are disallowed from discussing that the show was in fact a show or even having seen the show before. The residents also cannot discuss their life outside of the compound unless being told to do so. They can't harm another resident, and if they break any of the rules, they will be punished. On the big screen in the house, it will show the daily instructions for the contestants to carry out. They will be rewarded if everyone takes part. If successful, they would receive rewards such as food, furniture, appliances, or things that make their lives easier. There are personal tasks on the little screens that reward individuals. The tasks are unlimited, and a new one would appear every time one was completed. The reward will be lost if the task is not completed within a day.

As the competition progresses, some contestants are banished via voting, and some have to leave if they don't have a sleeping partner. Tension is brewing among the contestants within the compound and leads to violence. The producer sets up a fire and burns part of the compound as a punishment for the residents for breaking the rule. The incident has also become the test for the remaining residents' patience, and some of them begin to question their meaning of existence in the compound.

## Personal Thoughts
I found it mediocre and wish there were more backstory about the outside world of the compound. This book describes that the story takes place in a dystopian world. I kept searching for the dystopian side of the story, but it was vague as fog. I started to question what is considered enough to be in a "dystopian" state. Probably, my definition for "dystopian" is different than others. I was really disappointed towards the end because of the flat ending. I was hoping for something spectacular because the story was quite thrilling when it reached the last three contestants. In short, the ending is not an ending.
